Masculine & feminine — start here

This is the foundation English does not have, and everything builds on it. Every noun has a gender, and its ending signals gender and number. Get this and sentence construction follows; miss it and articles, adjectives and prepositions won't make sense.

Throughout this app, masculine is shown in violet and feminine in pink (kept separate from the verb-family colours).
EndingUsuallySingularPlural
-omasculinelibrolibri
-afemininecasacase
-eeithercane (m) · chiave (f)cani · chiavi

Plural rule: -o → -i, -a → -e, and -e words (either gender) → -i. This is a strong tendency, not a law — see the next panel for the exceptions and the endings that predict gender reliably.

Gender: exceptions & reliable clues

The -o/-a rule fails often enough to catch you out. Learn these early so you don't build the wrong habit.

Common exceptions

Looks like…But is…Examples
-amasculineil problema, il tema, il programma, il sistema, il clima, il cinema, il papa, il poeta
-ofemininela mano, la foto, la radio, la moto, la biro

Many words ending -ma (from Greek) are masculine.

Endings that DO predict gender

EndingGenderExamples

For people, gender is natural; nouns in -ista or -e can be both: il / la turista, il / la cantante. For any -e noun, learn it with its article.

Agreement in a sentence

Adjectives — and many past participles — must match the noun in gender and number, echoing the o/a rule.

MasculineFeminine
Singularil ragazzo altola ragazza alta
Plurali ragazzi altile ragazze alte

This drives the passato prossimo of essere-verbs: Marco è andato, Anna è andata, sono andati, sono andate.

Adjective position

Unlike English, most descriptive adjectives come after the noun.

  • After (the norm): una macchina rossa, un libro interessante, un vino italiano.
  • Before (a small common set): bello, buono, brutto, grande, piccolo, nuovo, vecchio, giovane, bravo — una bella casa, un buon amico.

A few change meaning by position:

BeforeAfter
un grande uomo — a great manun uomo grande — a big / tall man
una vecchia amica — a long-standing friendun’amica vecchia — an elderly friend

Either way, the adjective still agrees in gender and number.

Object & possessive pronouns

Direct object — "whom / what"

They replace the thing acted on and sit before the verb: mi, ti, lo / la, ci, vi, li / le.

Lo vedo = I see him / it.   La compro = I'm buying it (fem.).

Indirect object — "to whom"

mi, ti, gli / le, ci, vi, gli: Gli parlo = I speak to him; Le scrivo = I write to her.

Possessives

Normally used with the article and they agree with the thing owned, not the owner — so la sua casa is "his or her house".

The three verb families

Every verb belongs to one group by its infinitive ending. Drop the ending for the stem, then add the endings for each person and tense.

-are parlare   -ere credere   -ire dormire

These colours follow the verb through the app — each card in Reference carries a matching stripe. A large subset of -ire verbs (capire, finire, preferire…) inserts -isc- in the present tense only.

Endings that repeat

The shortcut that saves memorising three full sets. In the present, three persons take an identical ending in all families:

  • io → always -o (parlo, credo, dormo)
  • tu → always -i (parli, credi, dormi)
  • noi → always -iamo (parliamo, crediamo, dormiamo)

-ere and -ire also share the loro ending -ono. In the futuro and condizionale, -are and -ere are identical and -ire only swaps a vowel. Open any tense in Reference: shared-by-all cells are bold, shared-by-two are underlined.

Past · present · future

Passato prossimo and imperfetto are both past; futuro is the future. Condizionale ("would") and congiuntivo (subjunctive) are moods, not tenses — grouped after the "mood" divider in Reference.
  • Presente — parlo "I speak".
  • Passato prossimo — ho parlato. Present of avere/essere + participle (-are→-ato, -ere→-uto, -ire→-ito). The participle stays fixed; the auxiliary changes: ho / hai / ha / abbiamo / avete / hanno capito.
  • Imperfetto — parlavo "I was speaking / used to speak".
  • Futuro — parlerò "I will speak".
  • Condizionale — parlerei "I would speak" (vorrei = "I would like").
  • Congiuntivo — after penso che, voglio che…: che io parli.

Present continuous — stare + gerund

For "I am (right now) doing", use the present of stare + the gerund: -are → -ando, -ere & -ire → -endo.

Which verbs take essere

Most verbs form the past with avere. A limited set uses essere — and then the participle agrees with the subject. Learn the groups:

GroupVerbs
Movementandare, venire, arrivare, partire, uscire, entrare, tornare, salire, scendere, cadere
Stayingstare, restare, rimanere
Being / becomingessere, diventare, nascere, morire, crescere
All reflexiveslavarsi, alzarsi, svegliarsi… (mi sono lavato)

Examples: sono andato, è nata, siamo rimasti. Everything else: ho mangiato, ho visto, ho parlato.

Reflexive verbs

Verbs like lavarsi (to wash oneself) take a reflexive pronoun before the verb: mi, ti, si, ci, vi, si. The verb conjugates as normal.

PronounlavarsiEnglish
mimi lavoI wash (myself)
titi laviyou wash
sisi lavahe / she washes
cici laviamowe wash
vivi lavateyou (all) wash
sisi lavanothey wash

In the passato prossimo, reflexives always use essere, so the participle agrees: mi sono lavato / lavata.

Passato prossimo vs imperfetto

Both are past, but they answer different questions — the distinction that trips up most learners, so tackle it once the individual tenses feel comfortable.

Passato prossimo — what happenedImperfetto — the background
A finished, one-off actionOngoing, habitual or repeated
Ieri ho mangiato la pizza.
Yesterday I ate pizza.		Da bambino mangiavo sempre la pizza.
As a child I always ate pizza.
The event that happensDescriptions, time, weather, age, feelings
…è arrivato Marco.
Marco arrived.		Mentre leggevo…
While I was reading…
Rule of thumb: imperfetto sets the scene ("was doing / used to"); passato prossimo is the thing that then happened.
